Mathematical Foundations of Braids

1. What is a Braid?

Geometrically, an $n$-braid consists of $n$ non-intersecting strands in a tridimensional space connecting $n$ fixed points on a top horizontal bar to $n$ fixed points on a bottom horizontal bar.

The strands flow strictly downwards without back-tracking or looping back upwards. Two braids are considered equivalent if one can be continuously deformed into the other without cutting strands or passing strands through one another (a continuous deformation called ambient isotopy).

2. Braid Moves & Relations

By stacking one braid diagram on top of another, we define the composition of braids. Up to continuous deformation, any braid on $n$ strands can be decomposed into elementary cross-overs called generators $\sigma_i$.

The generator $\sigma_i$ represents crossing the $i$-th strand over the $(i+1)$-th strand, while $\sigma_i^{-1}$ represents crossing the $i$-th strand under the $(i+1)$-th strand.

These generators satisfy two fundamental algebraic relations (known as the Artin braid relations):

1. Far Commutativity: $$\sigma_i \sigma_j = \sigma_j \sigma_i \quad \text{for } |i - j| \ge 2$$
2. Braid Relation (Yang-Baxter Equation): $$\sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i = \sigma_{i+1} \sigma_i \sigma_{i+1} \quad \text{for all } 1 \le i \le n-2$$

3. What is a Group?

In abstract algebra, a group is a set $G$ equipped with a binary operation $\cdot$ (multiplication/composition) satisfying four fundamental axioms:

  1. Closure: For all $a, b \in G$, the product $a \cdot b \in G$.
  2. Associativity: For all $a, b, c \in G$, $(a \cdot b) \cdot c = a \cdot (b \cdot c)$.
  3. Identity Element: There exists an element $e \in G$ such that $e \cdot a = a \cdot e = a$ for all $a \in G$.
  4. Inverse Element: For every $a \in G$, there exists an inverse $a^{-1} \in G$ such that $a \cdot a^{-1} = a^{-1} \cdot a = e$.

4. The Braid Group $B_4$

The set of all $n$-braids under concatenation forms the Artin Braid Group $B_n$. In our game, we focus specifically on the 4-strand braid group $B_4$.

The group $B_4$ is generated by three elementary generators $\{\sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3\}$ and their inverses $\{\sigma_1^{-1}, \sigma_2^{-1}, \sigma_3^{-1}\}$. Its formal presentation is:

$$B_4 = \left\langle \sigma_1, \sigma_2, \sigma_3 \;\middle|\; \begin{array}{l} \sigma_1 \sigma_3 = \sigma_3 \sigma_1 \\ \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \sigma_1 = \sigma_2 \sigma_1 \sigma_2 \\ \sigma_2 \sigma_3 \sigma_2 = \sigma_3 \sigma_2 \sigma_3 \end{array} \right\rangle$$

4.1 The Symmetric Group ($S_4$)

If we ignore how strands cross over or under each other and only care about where each strand starts and ends, every braid induces a permutation of the 4 endpoints.

This defines a canonical surjection (homomorphism) $\pi: B_4 \to S_4$ onto the Symmetric Group $S_4$ (the group of permutations of 4 elements). Each generator $\sigma_i$ maps to the simple transposition $(i \quad i+1)$:

$$\pi(\sigma_1) = (1 \; 2), \quad \pi(\sigma_2) = (2 \; 3), \quad \pi(\sigma_3) = (3 \; 4)$$

While $S_4$ is finite with $|S_4| = 4! = 24$ elements, the braid group $B_4$ is infinite because strand wrapping and twisting are preserved!

5. Group Actions on a Set

A group action formalizes how an abstract group acts as transformations on a set $X$. Formally, a left action of $G$ on $X$ is a function $\cdot : G \times X \to X$ such that:

  • $e \cdot x = x$ for all $x \in X$ (identity action).
  • $(g \cdot h) \cdot x = g \cdot (h \cdot x)$ for all $g, h \in G$ and $x \in X$ (compatibility).

In Braid Game: The braid group $B_4$ acts naturally on the set of strand positions $X = \{1, 2, 3, 4\}$, as well as on game states (such as cards, players' scores, or topological invariants). Every time you play a generator card, you are applying a group action to transform the current braid state!
